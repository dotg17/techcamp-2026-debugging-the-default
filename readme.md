# Debugging the Default

**Why “Just Speak Up” is Bad Advice**  
Handout for techcamp Hamburg 2026  
Dorothee Greve

This repository contains the companion handout for my talk **“Debugging the Default: Why ‘Just Speak Up’ is Bad Advice”** at **techcamp Hamburg 2026**.

The handout turns the talk into small, practical moves for meetings and collaboration spaces that run on hidden defaults - especially when those defaults create cognitive, energetic, and emotional load that is not shared equally.

## What the handout covers

The handout follows four workplace defaults:

- **Speed** - the accelerating meeting
- **Specification** - the underspecified prompt
- **Decoding** - the moving screen
- **Being heard** - who gets to score

For each default, it offers small moves for:

- people carrying the load
- people running the room

It also includes a short research appendix for further reading.

## Core idea

> What looks like a personal struggle is often a design default doing its work.

Defaults are not neutral. They are built into culture, structures, processes, tools, calendars, screens, and meeting habits. Small moves, repeated often, can teach the room a new default.
